## The failure: Drive is revoked for boctor-financials

Production signs in — `mboctor@gmail.com`, v0.39.1. Spending renders and FY2025-26
renders: 2,964 transactions, $3,916,037 in, $3,852,232 out, full category breakdown
(INVESTMENTS $550,316.11 across 168 transactions, and so on). The Inbox loads, defaults
to FY2025-26, and shows 135 waiting.

**The document preview does not load.** The panel reports:

> Google Drive token expired and refresh failed. Please reconnect your account in
> Settings. Use "Open in Drive" above.

Diagnosed directly rather than guessed:

- Our stored grant in `bf_accounts` still *lists* `drive.readonly`, and a refresh token
  is present. The stored scope is only a record of what was granted; it does not prove
  the grant still exists at Google.
- The stored access token expired 2026-09-16, which is routine — that is what refresh is
  for.
- Exchanging our refresh token at `oauth2.googleapis.com/token` returns:
  **HTTP 400, `invalid_grant`, "Token has been expired or revoked."**

So Google has revoked the refresh token itself.

### Why DEC-4 is the likely cause

The two apps hold separate grants against the **same OAuth client**. DEC-4 had Maged
re-consent the hub today with a narrower scope set, dropping `drive.readonly`. A
re-consent that changes the granted scope set for a given client and user can invalidate
refresh tokens previously issued under the old set — including ones issued to the other
application.

The timing supports it. The Drive backfill ran successfully on 21 September, four days
ago, so this is not the six-month idle revocation. Nothing else changed on our side:
v0.39.1 touched drizzle-kit config, one test and the version files.

I cannot prove causation from the outside — Google does not say who revoked what — but
this is the failure mode, in the window, that DEC-4 predicted. Treat it as caused by
DEC-4 unless something better explains it.

**This is exactly the check added to TC-009 at our suggestion. It earned its place on the
first run.** Please record it as a found defect rather than a passing step.

## One thing to carry into the hub's own docs

The hub's CLAUDE.md-equivalent should say what boctor-financials' already says about
secret rotation, widened to cover this case:

> The two apps hold separate grants against the same Google OAuth client. **Any change to
> what either app requests — a rotated secret OR a changed scope set — can invalidate the
> other app's refresh token.** After such a change, BOTH apps must be signed out of,
> signed back into, and reconnected.

Rotating the secret was already known to do this. That a *narrowing of scopes* does it too
is new, and is the lesson from this deployment.
